Six countries, one admission axis

China, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, the United States and Vietnam each measure how hard a university seat is to win against a different pool of students. Every number here is pushed onto the same one: the country's age-18 population. From there on the subject is the best tenth of that population, everywhere except the chart of admission routes, which has to count everybody who enrolls.

What each country's percentile counts

A Vietnamese percentile counts only students who sat the THPT, a Thai one only students who finished ม.6. Whoever sat no test is assumed to rank below everyone who did.

pcohort = 100 × (1 − (1 − pnative/100) × B / P)

B is the pool the country's own percentile sits on, with repeat sitters removed so that it counts the people one cohort sends to the exam rather than everyone in the room; P is the age-18 population. A factor of 1.00 means the project already worked in age-cohort terms. Seats and universities count what these tables can see, not what the country admits.

What wins a university seat

Every published admission route, collapsed into four kinds of claim — a test score, a school record, someone's judgement, a special category — plus the transfer intake only the United States runs at scale. A route is a claim about everybody who enrolls, so this is the one section here that looks past the top tenth.

Transcribed from the coverage table in each project's README. The share beside each row is how much of an age-18 cohort those seats would fill, so the remainder enrolls somewhere this data cannot see, or nowhere; American transfers count a seat twice. China's row counts the Gaokao-scored seats this repository models, leaving 强基, 保送, the art and sport intakes, and 高职单招 outside.

Which fields spend their seats on the top tenth

Programs matched by name in each country's own language, then weighed by seats. A seat counts when its program's median enrollee stands in the top tenth of the cohort, which is where medicine puts nearly all of them wherever doctors are admitted straight out of school.

China names no majors, and the USA project scores a school rather than a degree, so the American row moves only with which schools teach what. Thai MD seats are settled in an earlier consortium round this data does not cover, so dentistry and pharmacy stand for medicine there.

Does the school or the major place a top-decile student?

Each dot is a program whose median student stands in the top tenth, at its university's whole-school standing across and its own standing up. Dots right of the dashed line sit in universities that are elite themselves. Dots left of it are elite programs inside ordinary universities, where the major did the work.

Do the most selective seats hold the best students?

Counting the universities that hold a country's best students only counts the country, so both axes here are shares of a cohort instead. Walk the universities down from the most selective, cumulate their seats against the age-18 population, and read off how good the students in those seats are. A perfect sort would put the top tenth of the seats in the top tenth of the cohort.

Top universities

Every country has a name that means the top: 清华, 서울대, จุฬาฯ, 臺大. What the name is worth differs, and once each median enrollee sits on the age-18 population the difference is a distance rather than an argument. The bar is logarithmic in the share of the cohort standing above a university, because the interesting differences live in the last percent.

How many students clear the bar for each seat behind it?

A school's bar lets in everyone above it, so the students competing for its seats are everyone in the cohort who clears that bar, and the seats they compete for are every seat behind a bar at least as high. A country that sorted perfectly would read 1.0 the whole way down. Everything above that is a candidate who clears the bar and does not get the seat.

How many people does a seat turn away once the scores are in?

Three of the six publish the width of the funnel against a program. Taiwan publishes the real thing — 個人申請 screens on 學測 down to a multiple of the intake and interviews whoever is left. Korea and Thailand publish applications per seat instead, one stage earlier, counting a student once per form filed.
