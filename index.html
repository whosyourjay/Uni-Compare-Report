01 · Coverage

Start with the populations behind the percentiles. Countries test different shares of a cohort, and these datasets cover different shares of their university seats.

Percentile populations

Population and coverage notes

B counts distinct cohort members in the assessment pool; P is the age-18 population. Hover or focus a B/P bar for its exact value. Seat, university, and program counts describe the available data, not national totals.

To compare cohort percentiles, the conversion places non-participants below participants. A factor of 1 means the source already uses the cohort scale.

pcohort = 100 × (1 − (1 − pnative/100) × B/P)

04 · Within universities

School or major?

Reading the dots and coverage

A dot represents a top-decile program. Its horizontal position shows its university's median; its vertical position shows the program's median.

Top-decile seats by field

Field definitions and coverage

Programs are classified by name and weighted by seats. The tooltip reports how many seats have an ability estimate.

China includes all recorded undergraduate plan seats. It uses major estimates where available, then existing exam-track estimates, then university estimates. Any seats still without an estimate stay in the denominator.

US undergraduate fields inherit their university's estimate; medicine and law use professional-entry cohorts. Russia's numeric comparison covers ЕГЭ-taking admits. Thailand's medical comparison covers dentistry and pharmacy, not the separate MD consortium.

Entering classes near NTU

What the curves estimate

Each curve combines score buckets or reported group centers, weighted by seats. A small blur on the horizontal scale softens discrete scores without changing the underlying estimates. Russia uses broad-field averages, not observed individual-score distributions.
